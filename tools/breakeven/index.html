In plain English. If an item costs £60 and sells for £100, a 20% discount cuts profit per kept item from £40 to £20. With equal return rates, you would need to sell twice as many to earn the same gross profit. Higher promotional return rates increase that hurdle.

02 — HOW TO USE IT

How to read the result

The estimator compares expected gross profit per order, then tells you how much extra volume is needed to reach parity.

1. The break-even hurdle

It compares profit per full-price order after returns with profit per promotional order after returns. The required uplift is the extra order volume that makes those totals equal.

2. Your scenario

Adjust the volume-uplift slider to compare your expected increase with the break-even hurdle. The result shows whether gross profit is above, below or at parity with full-price sales.

3. Important assumptions

Returned items are assumed recoverable at original cost. Shipping, handling, payment fees, markdowns on returned stock, overhead and capacity constraints are excluded; include them in your own analysis.

Example

With a 40% original margin and a 20% discount, the required uplift is +100% if return rates are equal and other costs are ignored. If promotional returns are higher, the required uplift rises. Use the controls above for your own values.
